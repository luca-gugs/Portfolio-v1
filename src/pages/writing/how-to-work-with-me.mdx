Hi, I'm Luca. I'm a founding engineer at RetailReady. This is how I work, what I expect from you, and what you can expect from me. If we're about to work together, read it.

## How I got here

I studied philosophy at NYU and loved the classwork, but I got burnt out on it. I couldn't see myself teaching or becoming a lawyer, which is where most of my classmates were headed.

I tried commercial real estate next. I was terrible at it. I was very shy back then (less so now), and it would take me hours to work up the courage for one cold call. I'd get rejected immediately and go cry in the bathroom.

Then my friends and I decided to build our own version of CoStar. None of us could code, so we paid a “highly touted” overseas dev team to build it. They stole our money.

After that we taught ourselves. PHP first (mistake), then JavaScript. I fell in love with building dumb websites for startup ideas we cooked up in our living room in the East Village.

In 2019 an engineer at EasyKnock, a proptech startup in NYC, got me in the door. I started out on what I'd call “React for babies”: styled-components (remember those?) and basic forms. So I kept begging for harder tickets, like the pricing engine and the internal tools our sales, underwriting, and portfolio teams lived in, and ate up whatever they'd give me. Looking back, it was my minor leagues.

I had an idea for a portal to walk customers through trading their mortgage for a lease and cash. I built it in stealth and pitched it to the C-suite. They bought in. We pulled in the sharpest PM I've ever met and a great designer, and ran it like a little startup inside the company. The Salesforce admins fought it. They thought all of that should keep living in a CRM. They were comically wrong. Looking back, I should have fought harder, but I was young and scared for my job. Thankfully a few people stuck up for us, and the best people there taught me how to move fast and how to actually care about the people using what I build.

After five years I felt ready for the majors: a founding team where ideas win on their merit, not on seniority. In 2024 I joined RetailReady as the first hire. We're 15 people now, and I'm working to change how brands and 3PLs ship product and make sales.

## How I work

### Honesty goes both ways

I believe in radical honesty and transparency. That includes uncomfortable truths and “I don't know.” I also believe in “strong opinions, weakly held.” If I've done the research, I'll tell you. If it's a gut call, I'll tell you that too.

I push my ideas hard. Don't let me push them all over you. Fight for yours and I'll fight for mine, and I promise I'll respect the debate. When you're right, I'll change my mind. The right answer matters more than whose answer it was.

Don't soften feedback for me either. The faster you tell me what I'm doing wrong, the faster I stop doing it.

### I'll teach you everything I know

I'll explain the why, not just the what, and I'll stick with it until it clicks. I expect the same back. When you figure something out, teach it to the rest of us.

### It's going to work out

I'm an optimist. When something breaks, my first assumption is that it's fixable and we're going to fix it.

### Read your code

Agents write most of my code now, which makes reading it more important, not less. If your name is on the PR, you should have read every line and be able to explain it. “The agent wrote it” is not an answer in code review.

### I give it everything

Work takes up most of our days, and life's too short to give it anything less than everything you've got. I take it home with me and stew on it at night. I expect the people I work with to do the same.

### I want to keep getting better

I put in time outside work to get better as an engineer and a product thinker. Every 6 to 9 months I want to look back at my old work and think “wow, I used to suck.” If I don't, I'm not growing fast enough. I expect you to be doing the same.

## What I expect from you

- Tell me the truth, especially when it's bad news.
- Push back when you think I'm wrong. Bring evidence.
- Teach what you learn.
- Bring the same commitment I do.
- Cringe at your own work from 6 to 9 months ago.

## What you can expect from me

- Direct feedback, fast.
- Transparency into how sure I am of a given take.
- Anything I know, whenever you ask.
- A real change of mind when you win an argument.
- Optimism when things go sideways.
- Everything I have.

## Let's talk

If you disagree with something on this page, tell me. That's kind of the point.
